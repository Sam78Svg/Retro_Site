Corporate History & Legacy

From humble beginnings to global leadership, Hisen Corporation's trajectory is marked by relentless dedication to quality and technological innovation. Over the course of six decades, we transitioned from local textile loom mechanics to pioneers of precision electronic components and global fiber-optic networks.

Our team of world-class engineers, technicians, and visionaries work tirelessly around the clock to solve complex industrial bottlenecks. Today, our reach spans over fifteen countries, servicing hundreds of Fortune 500 enterprises with state-of-the-art telemetry and raw materials processing. Read more about our values on the About Us page.


Foundation (1939)

In 1939, Hisen Corporation was formally registered with a simple, clear vision: deliver heavy industrial machinery built to last. Operating out of our first small foundry, our founder recognized that reliability was the ultimate differentiator. Despite the massive shifts in worldwide manufacturing, Hisen stayed true to this core design philosophy.

Throughout the late 1940s and 1950s, we diversified into chemical synthesis and rubberized electrical insulation—components that proved critical as the global economy surged forward into the computing age. Our foundational focus on customer reliability is still the engine that drives us forward today.


Historical Lineage Milestones
  • 1939: Founded in Pennsylvania as Hisen Ironworks, specializing in textile loom components and gear assemblies.
  • 1952: Opened the New Jersey Chemical Plant, entering plastics and heavy rubber insulation.
  • 1967: Launched the Hisen Instruments division to produce early electronic telemetry systems.
  • 1978: Developed our first high-capacity mainframe processing controller.
  • 1989: Restructured into Hisen Corporation, merging raw manufacturing with emerging communications technology.
  • 1995: Deployed our first corporate web portal on the World Wide Web.
  • 2001: Commenced commercial deployment of broadband systems and optical networks.
  • 2002: Released the enterprise portal v4.2 web standard for high-bandwidth corporate interactions.
